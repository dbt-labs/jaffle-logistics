WHY THE DATA TEAM

The team that owns the data now owns how good the company's AI gets.

ANALYTICS ENGINEERING→CONTEXT ENGINEERING
Model quality became answer quality. If the definition of "on time" is wrong in the model, every agent that reads it is wrong, and confidently so. Analytics engineering modelled data so a person could read it; context engineering models the same data so an agent can. Tests, documented definitions and contracts were good practice for BI; for AI they are essential.

THE SECOND CAUSE: ONE DOCK BAY

Not weather, not the fleet: handling damage at Amsterdam bay 3
DAMAGE INCIDENTS · HUB-AMS

Leading explanation: a dock-staging process fault (moderate confidence)
This is the second contributing factor in Jaffle Werke's 2025 on-time decline, beside the Paris storm (CRM review, 20 Oct). Vehicles and drivers are within ordinary variation, the maintenance record is ordinary, and it is the hub's own freight mix: the fleet is not the cause. It is a place. Bay 3 had no damage in 2023-24 and its staging dwell rose from about 46 to 70 minutes. Confidence is moderate, and the thing that would settle it is bay 3's equipment-inspection results. From Q4 on-time is tracked by cause and the hub runs an SOP audit; peak volume is kept off bay 3. Not measured: damage cost in euros. Damage rose at four of five hubs in 2025, so bay 3 is not the whole network's rise. The sources are messy: 14 of 15 incident reports name dock staging, and 5 reports cite IR-9003, 4 of them filed months before it existed, so free text needs provenance, not just retrieval. Read left to right. On 16 January 2025 the safety team filed IR-9001 for incident INC-2025-00417: during the 14 to 16 January storm a semi jackknifed on an icy on-ramp leaving the Paris hub, no injuries. It arrives as free text, so parsing here means shaping, not OCR: staging restores its line breaks and int_incident_reports_documents joins it to its shipment, which gives it an account, Jaffle Werke. split_incident_reports cuts it into 13 sentences and chunk_incident_reports packs them into 3 token-bounded passages that never span two reports. incident_reports_embed turns each passage into a vector with embed(), and checks each passage's content hash so one that has not changed is not embedded and paid for again. knowledge_base lands them as 3 rows with source_type Incident Report, the account, the text, the embedding and a citation back to IR-9001. None of the functions are the hard part: Snowflake, Databricks and BigQuery each ship parse, chunk, embed and search as SQL functions. The per-warehouse table is in the appendix. The engineering is the pipeline between the calls.

Six steps. A question arrives; the warehouse's tools answer over MCP, never a guessed number. There are two sources of truth, kept visibly apart: the semantic layer owns the number (10 governed metrics, the definition belongs to the business) and the knowledge base owns the cause (vector search over 7 document sources: transcripts, CRM notes, incidents, dispatch). The answer is the number and the cause: 56.60% on time, and the 14-16 January storm. Then the evidence: lineage back to the source system, and 340 tests per build. Then the action: a person decides, or an agent acts, for example routing a complaint, raising the service-level risk before the customer feels it, or stopping a dispatch when a driver's certification has lapsed. Be clear that this loop is a design, not a running system. Adapted from the context-layer pattern, not copied.

SAME CRAFT. A DIFFERENT READER.

From analytics engineer to context engineer
BEFORE
A PERSON READS
The deliverable is an answer
A chart. A dashboard. It answers once, then it is a dead end.
NOW
AN AGENT READS
The deliverable is the meaning
Definitions, grain, caveats, lineage. It answers every question downstream.
For a decade the work product was an answer: a chart, a dashboard. Now it is the meaning, because that is what an agent reads, and an agent cannot ask a clarifying question. A dashboard answers once; meaning answers every question downstream of it. The models are the same. The audience is not. The job changes when the reader changes; the craft does not. And you are already most of the way there: you write meaning down for a living, you own the semantic layer, you already run the gate an agent needs (tests, contracts, freshness, lineage), and you write in the one language both readers share, versioned code.

Identified issue leading to lower on-time performance
THE ISSUE
DAMAGE INCIDENTS · HUB-AMS
  • Leading explanation: a dock-staging process fault, at moderate confidence.
  • Bay 3: no damage in 2023–24; in 2025 its staging dwell rose from ~46 to 70 min and 11 of 15 incidents were found there.

WHAT IT MEANS

  • Discovered: only by joining four systems: the incident reports (14 of 15 name dock staging), Slack (#accounts: June's AMS incidents "don't fit that pattern"), the CRM's 20 Oct root-cause review and the bay record.
  • Next step: inspect bay 3's forklifts and staging lanes, and check how freight is staged there against the standard procedure. Until then, keep peak volume off bay 3.
